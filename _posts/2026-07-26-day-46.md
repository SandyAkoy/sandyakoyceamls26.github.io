reflection: |

 Today marked the conclusion of both the National Symposium on Effective and Ethical AI (NSEA) and an incredible nine-week summer research experience. Presenting our research project, "When Should a Biomedical Model Change Its Mind? A CFD-Guided Energy-Geometric Framework for Concept Drift in Multimodal Physiologic Signals," was one of the most rewarding moments of the program. I started my presentation feeling nervous, but once I reached my slide, I focused on connecting with the audience instead of worrying about my anxiety. Seeing people actively listen, ask thoughtful questions, and engage with our work made me realize how much I had learned throughout the summer. Presenting our hackathon idea afterward was equally exciting because it allowed us to demonstrate how AI could improve education through personalized learning and teacher support. I truly enjoyed seeing the creativity and dedication reflected in the projects presented by the other research teams, and it motivated me to continue exploring artificial intelligence beyond this program. The awards ceremony and closing remarks gave me the opportunity to reflect on everything I had accomplished over the past nine weeks. I am leaving this experience with new technical knowledge, stronger research skills, meaningful friendships, valuable professional connections, and much greater confidence in my abilities. This program has shown me that research is something I genuinely enjoy, and I can see myself continuing to pursue research opportunities when I return to the University of Houston. I am deeply grateful to Dr. Mack, Elijah, and Michael for believing in us, mentoring us, and creating an environment where I could learn, grow, and challenge myself. This summer has been one of the most impactful experiences of my academic journey, and I know the lessons and relationships I gained will continue to shape my future in technology and research.
