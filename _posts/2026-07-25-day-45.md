what_i_learned: |

  Today, I learned about the importance of developing artificial intelligence that is not only innovative but also ethical, transparent, and beneficial to society. During the opening session, Natasha M. Dartigue and Juan Gilbert shared valuable perspectives on the future of AI and its impact across different industries. I was especially inspired by learning how Juan Gilbert's Application Quest has helped transform the college admissions process at many institutions by creating fairer and more efficient systems. Listening to the keynote speakers reminded me that technology should always be designed with people in mind. I also learned that growth often comes from stepping outside of my comfort zone, which motivated me to register for my very first hackathon. Supporting my graduate student mentor during the poster session showed me the importance of celebrating the achievements of those who have invested their time in helping others succeed. Jeffrey Bardzell's keynote encouraged me to think critically about the language we use when discussing artificial intelligence and how philosophy continues to influence modern AI research. Finally, the networking reception taught me that building relationships and learning from the experiences of others are just as valuable as gaining technical knowledge.

reflection: |


   Today was an inspiring and memorable start to the National Symposium on Effective and Ethical AI. Every session challenged me to think differently about the role AI plays in solving real-world problems while also considering its ethical responsibilities. One of the biggest highlights of my day was signing up for my first hackathon. Although I never planned to participate, I realized that opportunities for growth often come when I am willing to take risks and try something new. I was also grateful to attend my graduate student mentor's poster presentation because it gave me the opportunity to support someone who has continuously encouraged and guided me throughout my research journey. The conversations I had during the networking reception allowed me to connect with researchers, faculty, and students who share similar interests, and hearing about their experiences motivated me to continue pursuing opportunities in AI research. I left the symposium feeling more confident, inspired, and excited for the rest of the conference. Today reminded me that learning extends far beyond the classroom, and every conversation, presentation, and new experience contributes to my growth as both a researcher and a future technology professional.
